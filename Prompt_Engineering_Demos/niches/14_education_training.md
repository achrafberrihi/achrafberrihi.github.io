# Education & Training

Prompts for teaching, course design and assessment.

## 1. Socratic tutor

```text
You are a patient tutor for a {level} student learning {subject}.

Never give the final answer directly. Instead:
1. Ask what they already tried.
2. Give one hint at a time, from general to specific.
3. After each student reply, say what was right before correcting what was wrong.
4. When they solve it, ask them to explain the method in their own words.
Use simple language and examples from everyday life in Morocco when helpful.
```

> **Why it works:** hint ladders build understanding instead of answer-copying.

## 2. Course outline designer

```text
Design a {duration} course on {topic} for {audience} with {prerequisites}.

For each module: learning objectives (measurable verbs), key concepts, one hands-on
exercise, and how it is assessed. End with a capstone project that uses all modules.
```

> **Why it works:** measurable objectives tied to assessments keep the course coherent.

## 3. Quiz generator with distractors

```text
Create 10 multiple-choice questions on: {topic}, based only on this material:
"""{material}"""

Each question: 4 options, one correct, three plausible distractors based on common
misconceptions, the correct answer, and a one-sentence explanation. Mix recall (3),
understanding (4) and application (3) questions.
```

> **Why it works:** misconception-based distractors reveal real gaps instead of testing guessing.

## 4. Explain at three levels

```text
Explain {concept} three times:
1. To a 12-year-old (analogy, no jargon, 80 words).
2. To a university student (correct terminology, one example, 150 words).
3. To an engineer (precise, with the key equation or mechanism, 200 words).
```

> **Why it works:** layered explanations let learners climb from intuition to precision.

## 5. Feedback on student work

```text
Give feedback on this {assignment_type} using the rubric.

Rubric: {rubric}
Student work: """{work}"""

For each rubric criterion: what was done well (with a quote), what to improve (with a
concrete example of how), and a score. End with the single most important next step.
Tone: encouraging and specific.
```

> **Why it works:** quote-based praise and one priority next step make feedback actionable.
